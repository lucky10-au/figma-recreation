How does Heaney present the power of nature in 'Storm on the Island'?The opening line 'We are prepared' suggests confidence and control, yet this assurance is quickly undermined as the poem progresses. The islanders build their houses squat and sink walls in rock, but these defences feel small against a force that 'dives and strafes invisibly'.

Furthermore, the simile of the 'tame cat turned savage' captures how quickly the familiar becomes threatening. The sea, which 'might' be company, offers no comfort at all. By the close, the speaker admits they 'are bombarded by the empty air', and fear itself becomes the subject: 'it is a huge nothing that we fear'. Nature here is not only physical but psychological, wearing down even the prepared mind.
